Five corrections from the user reshaped every prior assumption and must be carried forward into any future round. First, behavioral tracking is sharply limited: Mallan only sees engagement on its own surfaces. Clients also search StreetEasy, Zillow, Compass, Corcoran, Elliman and friends' agent feeds. What can be observed is email opens and clicks, Mallan's own listing pages and portal logins and calculator events and saves and shares and replies and SMS/calls through Mallan's numbers and document uploads and form submissions and showing requests. What cannot be observed is everything else. Don't design as if we see the whole web. Second, lead with circumstance, not behavior. For tenants and 1031 clients, the financial picture is mostly known at intake (lease applications carry income, credit, employment, savings, guarantor; 1031 clients' relinquished asset answers most questions). The 6-month wait isn't for data discovery — it's for rapport and timing. Approval Potential is the central computed score; "behavioral intent" is a derivative. Third, the lease-to-buy workflow is lease-start-relative, not lease-end-relative. Months 0–6 do nothing; tenant is settling. At month 6, run the qualification check, and if qualified, send listings similar to the unit they're already living in (not "their searched preferences" — they proved they like it by signing for it). If they engage, continue; if they request a showing, that's serious — escalate; if they open without contacting, soft resend every two weeks with a different angle each time, never the same email; about 90 days before lease expiration deliver the formal "have you considered buying?" ask; on a no, bifurcate to renewal or new-rental. Hypotheses are parallel and weighted, not exclusive — at any moment Sarah may be 0.20 likely to buy in her current building, 0.25 in a similar building, 0.10 condo for job-mobility flex, 0.15 co-op price-anchored, 0.20 renewal, 0.10 new rental; behavior reweights, system never picks. Fourth, fiduciary duty changes everything. Per RPL §443 and 19 NYCRR §175.7, agents owe LODCAR (Loyalty, Obedience, Disclosure, Confidentiality, Accountability, Reasonable care) only to clients, not customers. Agency state is per-person × per-transaction × per-agent. When a landlord is the client on a lease, the landlord wins on every lease decision; the tenant in that same lease is a customer with limited duties owed. The conflict gate runs before every send, recommendation, or draft and asks five questions: who is the agent's current client in this transaction; does this proposed action advance that client's interest; if it touches a non-client, does it conflict with the client's interest; does it use confidential information from another relationship; does it require agency disclosure that hasn't happened yet. Any yes on the last three is blocked or escalated. The information firewall is real: landlord-side confidential info — reservation rent, willingness to sell, view on Sarah, other tenants' info — cannot fuel tenant-side buy-track outreach. Lease application data needs Sarah's affirmative consent for re-use. Customer-to-client conversion is a five-step gate: customer-level outreach with role disclosure → engagement signal (showing request, "yes I want to talk", reply) → NY Agency Disclosure Form (statutorily required) → data-reuse consent (separate) → Buyer Representation Agreement establishing the client relationship. Until step five, Sarah is a prospective customer for buy purposes; advocacy language, confidential data leverage, and negotiation strategy generation are gated off. Fifth, don't replicate what vendors already provide. Boardpackager owns board-package assembly. Don't rebuild it. An external LMP is mandatory because REBNY does not grant LMP licenses to indie brokers. REBNY Connect NYC is free with membership; use it as an overlay. Tenant info is captured at application/board-package; don't ask twice. Mallan's job is consuming vendor outputs and turning them into intelligence and action — not data capture.

Nothing exists outside the graph.

The intelligence model has been corrected from the wrong original ("client behavior → infer intent → send listings") to the right model: intake plus qualification plus public records plus agent knowledge determine what's possible, Mallan engagement calibrates what attracts attention, search produces action plus reports plus comps plus next steps. Approval Potential, the central scoring concept, is a matrix per client: for Sarah Allen with $X income, $Y savings, Z credit, the matrix outputs that conventional 20%-down at $700K–$1M is 85% approvable, FHA 3.5% at $700K–$850K (condo only) is 92%, jumbo over $1M is 45% (liquidity falls short), all-cash is N/A, 333 East 46th's board is 58% (her own building, post-closing liquidity is the gating risk), 301 East 48th is 72% (line-mate option, likely), 240 East 47th is 88% (less strict reputation, strong), 240 Park Avenue is 12% (white-shoe, skip), standard condo approval is 92%, sponsor unit (no board) is 99% (easiest), lease renewal at current rent is ~95%, +5% rent is ~80%, +10% rent is ~50%. This is what the agent uses in every conversation; the data behind it is computable from circumstance plus public records, not behavior. Profile bootstraps from the current property: a new tenant is anchored on the unit they signed for; a new buyer (no lease) is anchored on first-five portal interactions; a closed buyer is anchored on the property they bought; a seller on the listing they're selling; a landlord on their unit's market position. The profile holds hard preferences (what they explicitly said), inferred preferences (what their behavior shows), friction themes (what's blocking them), stage (where they are in journey), momentum (heating up / steady / cooling), confidence (how much data we have), and drift (how much inferred ≠ hard). The most valuable column is Drift: when she said 2BR but only clicks 1BRs, that's price-anchoring, and the agent's call is "propose 1BRs with home-office potential." Profile decay uses half-lives — price preference 90 days (sticky), neighborhood 180 (very sticky), affordability stress 14 (volatile), friction themes 30 (resolvable), stage 7 (dynamic). Hypothesis distributions replace rigid state machines for ambiguous lifecycle stages, because nothing in NYC luxury real estate is binary; the same engine reads listings (engagement attractor scores, demand momentum, repeat-viewer count, friction themes, comp position, photo-quality signal, days-of-attention) so the seller portal and pricing-talk script and next-best-action engine all read the same primitive from a different angle. Cross-side discovery is just signal alignment: a tenant in 333 E 46th has inferred "Midtown East / doorman / 1BR / ~$1.1M / no pre-war"; a new sponsor unit launches in the neighbor at $980K; the system fires the match before the tenant ever opens the portal that morning. Same engine reads how a seller behaves on their own dashboard (cadence of checks, which metrics they linger on, comp-update reaction, feedback-theme reaction, price-flex signals, urgency posture), so the agent gets a "this seller is ready to talk price" task before the seller calls. Same for landlords (vacancy tolerance, concession openness, rent positioning vs market, renewal posture, capex appetite, disposition signals). Confidence and decay mean the system knows what it doesn't know — a profile with two clicks last week is low-confidence and the system says so explicitly, gates the recommendation, asks the agent to run the explicit-preferences form or wait for more interactions.

The decision engine runs two state machines in parallel per person. The lifecycle state machine moves them through new_lead → qualifying → active_pipeline → contract → closed → nurture_30d/90d/yearly → dormant → reactivated. The autonomy state machine moves through manual_only → approve_queue (every send agent-approved, default for active clients) → nurture_auto (pre-certified templates fire on schedule for closed clients in good standing) → reactivation_auto (lighter cadence for dormant clients) → paused (agent override). This is the user's "Option C" — autonomy is itself a state, driven by lifecycle stage and recent behavior, with explicit promotion rules that flip a closed client back to approve_queue when any of these fire: three-plus sale-listing opens in 30 days, mortgage calculator opened, click on "your home value" in a market report, reply to any nurture email (always escalate), ACRIS deed activity in their building, three-plus units in their building list or close in 60 days, or a listing matching their inferred preferences hits the feed and last activity wasn't dormant-flat. Triggers come in seven flavors: time-based (180/90/60/30-day windows), threshold-based (score crosses a number), event-based (calculator opened with custom inputs), drift-based (inferred ≠ hard by more than X), inactivity-based (no engagement N days), external-data-based (ACRIS, DOB, probate, divorce signals), and cross-entity (new match in graph). Eighth, added when fiduciary surfaced: disclosure-gate-required, which surfaces disclosure events themselves as next-best-actions. The send authorization gate composes a draft via the AI co-pilot, runs the compliance check, looks up `person.autonomy_state`, and either returns to agent for composition (manual_only), enqueues for one-tap approve (approve_queue), sends if template is on the pre-certified list (nurture_auto), light-touch sends with volume cap (reactivation_auto), or drops (paused). Behavioral response updates profile and may flip autonomy state. Eight state machines run on the same engine: buyer journey, tenant journey, seller journey, landlord journey, listing lifecycle, lease lifecycle, closed-client nurture, cross-side opportunity. They fire ripple events at each other — when Sarah's tenant.lease_active enters its 180-day window, the listing.projection_window opens on her unit and the landlord-side dashboard updates with "tenant Sarah, lease ends Apr 2027, buy-readiness 67, conversion probability 40%, renewal 30%, vacancy 30%, replacement-rent estimate $4.6K, replacement-tenant pool 14 Mallan renters matching." Same engine, parallel outcomes; one expiring lease becomes potentially three commissions (tenant buys with us, landlord sells or re-leases with us, replacement tenant we represent moves in).

Sarah's worked example, with corrected timing, illustrates the whole stack at once. Lease starts month 0. Months 0–6 the system does nothing — tenant settling phase — but the qualification matrix is computed on day 1 from the lease application and sits in the agent's daily brief showing "Sarah at 333 E 46th, lease just started, buy-qualified for FHA condo $700–$850K, eligible for buy-track at month 6." Month 6 the qualification check fires using only data Sarah has consented to share for buy purposes plus public/portal-derived signals — does NOT use lease application data without consent. Output is rougher initially, that's the point. The Layer 1 send fires with customer-level framing: *"Sarah, hi — Maya here from Mallan. We represent your landlord on the lease at 333 E 46C, so we know the building well. I've been seeing some interesting buy opportunities in your line and neighborhood lately, and wanted to share a few in case you're ever curious about ownership. No pressure and no obligation — if you'd like to explore seriously, I'd be glad to represent you as a buyer in a separate engagement, and we'd start that with the standard agency disclosure. Either way, here are 4 listings I thought you might find interesting…"* Compliance pre-flight: customer disclosure language present, no landlord-confidential info leveraged, no advocacy language, listings factual not pitched, TCPA consent captured at lease signing, Fair Housing scan, FARE Act not applicable, agency disclosure line present. Agent approves with one tap. Months 6–9 Sarah engages. Customer-level service continues. Soft resends every two weeks with no advocacy language. The system tracks her hypothesis distribution but the send drafts never cross into representation language until the disclosure form is signed. Month 9 the showing request lands — customer-to-client conversion trigger. Decision engine produces the next-best-action: schedule the showing AND present NY Agency Disclosure Form before the showing; the showing doesn't happen as a buyer-client event until disclosure completes. After conversion (steps three through five complete), Sarah is now a buyer-client; new agency state CLIENT × FUTURE_BUY × AGENT_MAYA. Different rules apply — full LODCAR duties owed, confidentiality flag set, AI co-pilot now drafts with advocacy language, negotiation playbooks become available, comp analysis interpretation becomes available — and Maya still owes the landlord on the lease; those duties don't vanish, they coexist. The dual-coexistence test runs continuously: Sarah wants to break the lease early to close 30 days early on a new place would harm the landlord and is blocked; Sarah closes after lease ends naturally is fine; Sarah asks "what's the lowest the landlord would accept on renewal?" is a confidential-info request from a customer-turned-client about the other party's confidential info and the AI draft cannot include the answer. If conflict becomes unavoidable (Sarah wants to buy from a seller Maya also represents), the system requires dual agency with informed written consent from both, or designated agency (not currently possible at single-agent Mallan, possible once team grows), or a refer-out. Alternate branch where Layer 1 is silent at month 7: the engine reads which dimensions she ignored — if she skipped all co-op listings, Layer 2 is condo-only; if she ignored everything, Layer 2 is rent-vs-buy calculator and FHA explainer educational content; if she opens neither, Layer 3 at month 9 is the renewal track and the friction-theme "wasn't ready, watch year 2" gets logged for next cycle.
